# Captions for Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts

Short-form video is watched with the sound off, and the caption style that holds attention is a few big words at a
time, with the word being said highlighted. OpenCaptions makes that from your own video, on your iPhone or Mac or on
your own server, with no subscription.

## Pick a look

Five looks are free in the app: **Bold** (heavy type, each word popping as it is said), **Purple Punch** (a coloured box
slides from word to word), **Karaoke** (the colour sweeps across each word), **Paper** (the word being said is
highlighted) and **Plain** (no animation). Pro adds fourteen more, such as **Boom**, **Neon** and **Slam**. Every look
is a starting point: the [style panel](../guides/styling.md) changes the font, the colours, the outline and how many
words show at once.

## Place the caption

The platforms draw their own buttons and text over the picture, mostly near the bottom and on the right edge. Drag
the caption away from those areas, and look at the saved video in the platform's own app before you post. In
OpenCaptions you drag it where you want it, and it snaps to the centre lines and the margins.

## Fix the words

Speech models miss names, brands and slang. Double-tap a word on the video to correct it before you save: a caption
that gets a name wrong is worse than none.

## Save for posting

- **Format:** MP4 (H.264) plays everywhere and is the usual choice.
- **Size:** 1080p is the free app's limit. For a vertical video it is 1080 by 1920, the usual size for this kind of video.
- **Frame rate:** 30 fps is the free app's limit. Pro adds 60 fps and 4K.
- **The mark:** the free app puts a small mark in the corner. Pro removes it, with a single purchase and no
  subscription.

## Make several at once

The `opencaptions` command line captions one video per call, with the same looks, so a script can loop over a
folder. Claude can do the same from a sentence: see [From Claude and other agents](../guides/agents.md).
